Exposure badges

Iconography prototype for the exposure spine — one address stack (NAME · HOST · PORT · PATH · SINK), one cell highlighted by position (which layer) and colour (how open). The six model kinds are just this stack with different cells bound / wildcarded (∗) / absent. Raw SVG, for iterating before Hylograph.

The openness ramp (colour of the lit cell)

all-interfaces · internetinternal netlocalhost · none

Warm = wide-open inbound surface (earns attention — it's what the world can hit); cool/grey = sealed. Note *:8080 (all interfaces) and 127.0.0.1:9000 (loopback) are the same HostPort kind but opposite ends of the ramp — the glob says what the enum can't.

Design C — vertical stack (conserves horizontal space)

Same five-cell glob, stacked top (world) → bottom (code). Narrow footprint — for when horizontal room is the scarce axis (wide-but-short nodes, dense columns).

situationvertical glob

Design D — concentric rings (conserves both axes; openness = radius of the lit ring)

The literal onion. Outer ring = world, inner = code; the lit ring's radius reads as the exposure spine directly (outer lit = exposed, inner lit = sealed) — so "how open is this?" is legible at a glance even when the detail isn't. Tiny footprint → the macro/overview glyph for hundreds of nodes, with cells/track as the detail-on-interrogation forms (a level-of-detail pair). The cost, as expected: which-layer and the literal value are hard to read small.
