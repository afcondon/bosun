# Brunel: the durable fixes — a session-drivable plan

*Companion to `BRUNEL-port-shadow-and-health.md`, which is the incident spec
(what happened, why, and the fix options). This is the pick-up-and-go plan:
current state, what the evidence now supports, and the three pieces of work with
anchors and acceptance criteria. Written 2026-08-05, after a second instance of
the same defect was found live.*

**Start here:** read `BRUNEL-port-shadow-and-health.md` §1–2 first (ten minutes),
then §2 below for what changed since it was written. The stopgap is fully
applied and the rig is healthy — nothing here is on fire. This is the work that
makes the stopgap unnecessary.

---

## 1. Current state — the stopgap is complete

`§5`'s rule is *"Atlantis-supervised / externally-run ⇒ `startCommand: null` in
the serve fleet, or serve binds the port and shadows it."* As of `d59f1ae` it is
applied to every Atlantis member that appears in `registry/fleet.json`:

| fleet id | port | service | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 159 | 3024 | Amphora | 2026-08-03, the original incident |
| 169 | 20808 | link-spike | 2026-08-03 |
| 158 | 3023 | Triggerfish frontend | 2026-08-05 — **was live-shadowed** |
| 167 | 3012 | purerl-tidal | 2026-08-05 |
| 170 | 57130 | es9-daemon | 2026-08-05 (also a port correction) |

**Known-fragile, by construction.** Three ways this regresses without touching
any code: `fleet.json` is regenerated by chair-server (`:3022`) registration, and
a rewrite can clobber the nulls; a newly-added Atlantis service starts life with
a real `startCommand` and is shadowed until someone remembers; and nothing
anywhere enforces or even checks the rule. It is a convention held in a human's
head, which is what makes it a stopgap rather than a fix.

**Left undone deliberately.** Fleet ids **163–166, 168** (Chair frontend,
chair-server, `supervise`, `serve`, Jurist) carry the same prose-in-
`startCommand` pattern but sit outside the Atlantis rule, and none was shadowed
when checked. Extending the rule to them is a judgement call, not a close-out.
Also, **`:57120` now has no fleet row at all** — id 170 moved to its true port
57130, and SuperDirt, which actually holds 57120, has never been registered.
That wants a chair-server registration, not an id invented by hand.

## 2. What the evidence now supports

The original note had one incident. There are now three, and the third is from a
different subsystem — which changes how the middle defect should be framed.

**(i) Amphora `:3024`, 2026-08-03.** serve on `127.0.0.1:3024` beside Amphora's
`0.0.0.0:3024`, no `EADDRINUSE`, serve tried to lazy-spawn a second Amphora and
hung. Loud: every request through `localhost` failed.

**(ii) Triggerfish `:3023`, found 2026-08-05.** The same shadow — serve on
`127.0.0.1:3023` beside `static-httpd`'s `*:3023` — but it had been running that
way for days **without anyone noticing**, because `static-httpd` binds **IPv6**.
Anything resolving to `::1` reached the real server; only the IPv4 path went
through serve's proxy hop. Measured: **110 ms via `127.0.0.1` against 0.7 ms via
`[::1]`**, both returning 200. After nulling + `SIGHUP`, IPv4 fell to 0.86 ms.

> The lesson worth carrying: **a shadow does not have to hang to be real.** (i)
> announced itself; (ii) was a 150× latency tax that looked like a working
> service. The diagnostic is not "does it respond" but "compare `curl` latency on
> `127.0.0.1` vs `[::1]` vs the LAN IP, and check `lsof` for two listeners."

**(iii) SuperDirt, 2026-08-05 — a different subsystem, same defect.** `bosun
supervise` reported `superdirt: running` while its `scsynth` was dead and
SuperDirt was deaf. Cause: `probe: process` watches the wrapper script
(`boot-superdirt.sh`), and the wrapper was alive. Nothing observed the thing a
client actually uses.

**So the general defect is broader than §2b states.** It is not "health is
inferred from port-binding" — it is:

> **Liveness is inferred from a proxy signal (a bound port, a live parent
> process) rather than observed at the interface the client actually uses.**

serve and supervise have independently grown the same bug in different forms.
Whatever lands for §2b should cover both, or it will be fixed twice.

## 3. The work

### 3a. Adoption must not depend on `EADDRINUSE` *(prevents the shadow)*

**Defect.** `cli/src/Bosun/CLI/Serve.js:37` — adoption fires only from
`server.on("error")` with `err.code === "EADDRINUSE"`. serve binds
`INTERNAL_HOST = "127.0.0.1"` (`Serve.js:19`, `:50`), so a `0.0.0.0` incumbent —
the normal case — raises no error under `SO_REUSEADDR` and adoption never runs.
The safety mechanism is defeated by the commonest bind configuration it exists to
protect.

**Approach — option (A) from the spec, pre-bind liveness probe.** Before
`listen`, attempt a TCP connect; if it connects, an incumbent exists, so adopt
without ever binding.

**The machinery already exists.** `waitForPort(port, timeoutMs)` at
`Serve.js:225` is exactly this connect-with-timeout probe, written for backend
readiness. Fix 3a is largely *calling it earlier and on a different address*, not
new I/O code.

**Seam.** Keep the verdict pure, the socket at the edge (per §4). The probe
result comes *in* to the planner; the planner emits adoption as a typed outcome.
Concretely, in `core/src/Bosun/Serve.purs`:

- `Admission` (line 129) is `Admit Route | Redir Redirect | Reject Rejection`.
  Add an adopted outcome — either a fourth constructor or an `external :: Boolean`
  on `Route` (line 83). Prefer the constructor: `Route` is consumed by the proxy
  path, and an adopted route has no backend to proxy *to*, so a flag invites
  every consumer to forget the case.
- `servePlan` (line 131) and `arbitrate` (line 148) must stay total. Note
  `arbitrate` already rejects a *second in-fleet* claimant with `PortClaimed`
  (line 99); that dedups **within the plan**, while the probe dedups **against
  the world**. They compose and neither replaces the other.
- Probe results must be an *input* to `servePlan`, not something it performs.

**Conformance.** The planner rides node ≡ Go differential conformance
(`conformance/`, `scripts/go-conformance.sh`). Any new `Admission` variant or
`Route` field must keep both sides byte-identical. Fixtures needed, under
`fixtures/serve/`: `0.0.0.0` incumbent, `127.0.0.1` incumbent, `::1` incumbent,
no incumbent — the four cases §4 names.

**Acceptance.** Reproduce §3's script (a `0.0.0.0` incumbent on 9999, then a
serve-style `127.0.0.1` bind): serve must report the route adopted/external and
bind nothing. Then restore a real `startCommand` to fleet id 158 while
`static-httpd` is running, reload, and confirm serve adopts `:3023` instead of
shadowing it — i.e. the stopgap is no longer load-bearing.

**Also consider (option C).** A periodic re-probe, so an incumbent that appears
*after* serve bound is adopted on the next tick rather than serve owning the port
forever. Incident (ii) is the argument for it: serve started first at boot.

### 3b. Health must be observed, not inferred *(detects the whole class)*

**Defect, restated per §2 above.** Two present forms:

- `Serve.js:97` — `up: s.external ? true : !!s.child`. Liveness is "did I spawn a
  child / is the port bound", never "does the endpoint answer".
- Atlantis `probe: process` — liveness is "is the wrapper process alive". This is
  what let SuperDirt report `running` while deaf.

**Approach.** A protocol-appropriate probe of the real endpoint: HTTP `GET` on a
configured readiness path returning 2xx within a timeout for HTTP services; a
protocol ping for others.

**The honest-floor rule matters and is easy to skip.** Services with no HTTP
surface — sockets, UDP daemons, `es9-daemon`, `fh2-daemon`, `continuo`,
SuperDirt — cannot always be probed meaningfully. Where there is no real check,
"port bound" or "process alive" is the honest floor, but it **must be reported as
such** (`bound, not probed`) and never rendered as `running`. Half this bug is
the reporting, not the checking: in all three incidents the *display* asserted
more than the evidence supported.

**The inverse failure is already recorded.** `ROADMAP.md:99` notes that nearly
every container on the mini reports `unhealthy` for days while serving 200s —
healthchecks calling `wget`/`curl` that a minimal image doesn't contain. Same
theme from the other side: a health signal that isn't measuring the thing it
claims to. A probe model worth building has to make both failures hard — false
`running` (incidents i–iii) and false `unhealthy` (the containers) — which in
practice means the probe's own failure mode must be distinguishable from the
service's.

**Scope decision to make first.** Whether this lands once (a shared probe model
in `core/`, consumed by both serve and supervise) or twice. Given (iii), once.
That is a bigger change than the spec assumes and is the main reason to schedule
this deliberately rather than squeeze it in.

**Acceptance.** Kill `scsynth` under a live `sclang` and confirm the Atlantis
group reports something other than `running` within a bounded interval. Point a
route at a process that binds but never answers and confirm the Chair does not
call it running.

**Concrete probe for SuperDirt, when you get there.** It answers OSC on UDP
57120; a UDP `/dirt/play` with a silent sample, or checking that `scsynth`
appears as a child of `sclang`, both beat the current wrapper check. The second
is cheap and needs no audio.

### 3c. Bind-family convention *(removes the masking)*

**Defect.** The fleet mixes families — `static-httpd` binds IPv6 `*:3023`,
Amphora IPv4 `*:3024`. No single host address reaches both, so "just use the
tailnet URL" is an unreliable escape hatch.

**Promoted from cosmetic.** The original note ranked this last, as a failed
workaround during diagnosis. Incident (ii) shows it is worse than that: the IPv6
bind is **what made the shadow invisible for days**. Mixed families do not merely
complicate debugging, they hide the very defect 3a exists to prevent. Cheapest of
the three and it removes a whole class of "works for me".

**Approach.** A fleet-wide convention — dual-stack, or a declared family per
service that tooling honours — so a service's public address is predictable.

**Acceptance.** For every fleet service, `127.0.0.1`, `[::1]` and the LAN IP
agree on both reachability and latency (no silent proxy hop on one family).

## 4. Suggested order

1. **3c** — cheapest, and until it lands you cannot trust a probe result on one
   family to mean anything on another. It also makes 3a's fixtures honest.
2. **3a** — retires the stopgap and the convention nobody can be relied on to
   remember.
3. **3b** — the largest, and the one worth doing properly rather than quickly:
   settle the serve/supervise scope question before writing code.

3b is the one with three incidents behind it, so there is a case for taking it
first. The reason not to: it is the only one whose design is genuinely open, and
doing 3c and 3a first shrinks it (a predictable address makes a probe simple to
specify).

## 5. Open questions to settle before coding

- Does the adopted outcome become a fourth `Admission` constructor or a `Route`
  field? (Recommended: constructor — see 3a.)
- Does the health model live once in `core/` for both serve and supervise, or
  separately? (Recommended: once — see 3b.)
- Where does the durable form of the §5 rule belong — enforced in the registry
  *source*, or made unnecessary by 3a? If 3a lands properly, an already-running
  service is adopted regardless of its `startCommand`, and the rule dissolves.
  Confirm that before building enforcement that 3a would render dead.
- Should chair-server refuse to write a `startCommand` for a service that is a
  member of a supervised group? That is the "clobbered on regeneration" hole, and
  it is independent of 3a.
